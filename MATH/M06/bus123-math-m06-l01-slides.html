Your task: choose a cost rule and explain your reason to a partner. No calculation needed. Which choice would leave more gross profit?

Your choices have names

Your cost ruleInventory methodFor these rising purchase costs
Older purchase cost firstFIFO

What you were evaluating: the rule for assigning purchase costs to goods sold—not a pricing or supplier decision.

Why it matters: the same $80 sale can have different assigned costs and gross profit. The costs not assigned to sales remain with the inventory still on hand.

These are cost-assignment rules. They do not necessarily describe which physical bag leaves the shelf.

Three ways to assign costs to a sale

Which purchase costs become COGS (cost of goods sold) when we sell a product?

FIFO

First In, First Out

Oldest purchase → cost sold first
Middle purchase
Newest purchase

Oldest costs go to sales first.

Newer costs remain in inventory.

LIFO

Last In, First Out

Oldest purchase
Middle purchase
Newest purchase → cost sold first

Newest costs go to sales first.

Older costs remain in inventory.

Weighted average

Often shortened to WAC

Oldest purchase ↘
Middle purchase → blend costs
Newest purchase ↗

Blend costs by quantity. Larger purchases count more.

Use the average for sold and unsold units.

External view: classify costs under the applicable reporting rules.
