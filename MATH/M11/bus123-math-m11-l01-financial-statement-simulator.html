Model assumptions

The simulation adds the MRI purchase and any borrowing to the workbook’s reporting period. The loan proceeds arrive before the purchase. There are no new taxes, interest, or repayments. The same supplies are purchased and used under every costing method. The change in supplies expense flows through cost of services. The starting operating cash flow already reflects the cash paid for those supplies.

Harborside Medical Scenario Log

Run these four scenarios in order. Click Record current scenario once per run, then answer its question in the Student explanation column.

  1. Buy the MRI with cash. Set the loan and new depreciation to $0 and select weighted average. How far is ending cash below $150,000? Which cash flow line shows the purchase?
  2. Find the minimum loan. Keep depreciation at $0 and use weighted average. Find the smallest loan that leaves at least $150,000 in ending cash. Confirm that borrowing $1,000 less misses the target, then restore and record the minimum loan. Which balance sheet and cash flow lines move when Harborside borrows?
  3. Record $20,000 in depreciation. Keep the minimum loan and weighted average. How much do net equipment and net income change? Why does ending cash stay the same?
  4. Switch to FIFO. Keep the minimum loan and $20,000 in depreciation. How much do ending inventory, cost of services, and net income change? Why does ending cash stay the same?
