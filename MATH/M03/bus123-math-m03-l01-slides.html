Bridge from M01 and M02

A discount changes what remains

A 30% discount removes 30% of the list price. The buyer pays the 70% complement.

30% off
70% paid
100% − 30% = 70%

The complement is the bridge from discount rate to net price.

From the Pre-Reading

Two formulas answer two questions

QuestionExcel PatternMeaning
What does the buyer pay=B9*(1-C9)*(1-D9)*(1-E9)Multiply the list price by every complement.
What single discount is equivalent=1-((1-C9)*(1-D9)*(1-E9))Subtract the combined complement from 1.

Decision check: a 20/10/5 chain leaves a 68.4% combined complement, so its single equivalent discount is 31.6%—not 35%.

Common Mistake

Savings is not the payment

Wrong conclusion

$4,200 × 2% = $84

“Send $84.” The calculation found only the dollars saved.

Correct conclusion

$4,200 − $84 = $4,116

Send the remaining invoice amount after the savings.

Name the output before trusting it: discount amount, net price, or payment?

Key Takeaways

Three habits protect the decision

01

Use the complement to move from a discount rate to the portion the buyer pays.

02

Multiply chain complements in sequence; never add successive discount rates.

03

Test the payment date before applying a cash discount to the invoice.
